Turn Rough Drafts into Polished Text with On-Device AI

By MooduTools September 21, 2026 6 min read

Most writing happens in two stages: capturing a rough idea, then refining it. The capture part is easy. The polishing part — finding the right tone, cutting the fluff, turning a jumble of notes into a clear summary — is where the real work lives. That is exactly where a private, on-device AI text tool shines.

The best part is that it runs entirely in your browser — no installation, no Chrome flags, and no sign-up. Rewriting, tone-shifting, and summarizing are powered by a lightweight in-browser WebGPU LLM (WebLLM) that runs on your own GPU in any WebGPU-capable browser, so your drafts never leave your machine. Your private writing stays private, refined as text that only you and your screen will ever see.

Start with a Clear Prompt

Good output starts with a good prompt. Instead of asking for something vague, name the persona, the goal, the constraints, and the format. For example: “Act as a senior marketing editor. Rewrite this as a concise, warm email for a loyal customer, under 120 words, ending with a single call to action.” A prompt builder turns your rough idea into that complete, high-quality request in one step.

Let the Model Find Your Tone

Tone is the difference between a message that lands and one that falls flat. The same raw sentences can read as firm, warm, or diplomatic depending on the words you choose. A tone shifter keeps your meaning intact while adjusting the voice — whether you need a polite business tone, a gentle rejection, or something direct but still courteous.

Summarize Without Losing the Point

Long documents, meeting notes, and dense articles all benefit from a clean summary. An on-device summarizer pulls out the core points — three-line takeaways, plain-language explanations, or lists of concrete next actions — so you can act on the information instead of drowning in it.

Why Do It Locally?

Handling your drafts on-device is a structural guarantee, not a promise. When text is processed locally, there is nothing for a company to collect: no upload bar, no server logs of your ideas, no training dataset quietly built from your writing. Private by architecture, not by policy.

Try it: open the text tools, paste a draft, and watch it get polished — locally, privately, and for free.
